<mark class="highlight">I'm <a href="https://yad.codes">Yad Konrad</a>.</mark> I started this repo on 31 December 2016 as a list of the talks and courses I was learning reinforcement learning from: Joelle Pineau's and Pieter Abbeel's 2016 talks, Sutton and Barto's book, David Silver's course, and Berkeley's CS 294. It has grown into notes, a lecture series, tested exercises, and this site.

## How I got here

1. **Self-driving in GTA V.** My way into RL was getting a car to drive itself in GTA V. That became [Nvidia-Autopilot-Keras](https://github.com/0bserver07/Nvidia-Autopilot-Keras) (January 2017): a Keras version of NVIDIA's end-to-end self-driving model ([Bojarski et al. 2016, arXiv:1604.07316](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.07316)), trained on a GTA V driving dataset from DeepDrive.
2. **Berkeley.** CS 294 Deep Reinforcement Learning, spring 2017, taught by Sergey Levine, John Schulman, and Chelsea Finn. [My notes from it](/notes/cs294-2017/) are kept as I wrote them.
3. **Then** program synthesis, then NLP with RL, then LLMs, and then other things.

Along the way:

- **David Silver's course.** DeepMind's David Silver taught the UCL course (2015) that was on the first version of this list. All ten lectures are on the [Watch](/watch/) page.
- **OpenAI Gym.** I worked on and contributed to Gym environments, under a GitHub account I don't have anymore.

The lecture series runs in a similar order: classical RL first, then RL for language models.

## How this was made

- The 2016 list of talks, books, and courses and the 2017 CS 294 notes are mine, written while I was learning. They stay as written.
- The 34 lectures, the exercises, the cheat sheets, and this site were drafted with AI coding assistants and then edited. Every lecture is marked unreviewed until a person has read it end to end and checked it. The exercises are tested against reference solutions.
